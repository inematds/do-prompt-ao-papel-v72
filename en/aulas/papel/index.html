Lesson 4 · 16 min

The role era: brilliant, but new to the company

Anthropic, the company that makes Claude, recommends treating Claude like a brilliant but new employee who doesn't know your rules yet. Anyone who has hired people knows what that takes: a clear goal, the background of where it came from, and an example of work done well. In this lesson you put the three together into a role sheet and check your sheet right on the page.

    Context

    Where this goal came from and why: who asked for it, who it's for, what has already been tried. Most of it comes from the base in lesson 3; in the sheet you just point to where it is.

  3. 3/5

    Example

    "Do a great job" doesn't work: it doesn't know what great means to you. Do the task once yourself, done well. It's the best-spent time, and probably the last time you do this task.

  4. 4/5

    Role sheet

    The sheet brings the three steps together and adds two things every employee needs: what they must never do (boundary) and when you check the work. This is the sheet you hand to the agent.

  5. 5/5

    From request to role

    A measurable goal, context and an example, plus a boundary and a check: the role sheet. You stop writing the work and start managing it.

A well-made sketch and a check once in a while: that is how you delegate to someone capable.

02 · How Anthropic's teams make requests

Cases told by Anthropic itself. Notice how short the requests are.

1. Rewriting the app

The creator of Claude Code (a programming agent) wanted the desktop app rewritten in Swift, another programming language.

The request came with its own test: run the old app, take a screenshot, compare the screens point by point with the new one, and don't stop until they match. The agent ran on its own for over two weeks.

Why it worksThe goal measures itself: the new screen is either the same as the old one or it isn't.

2. Replying on Slack

In another case, Claude joined a Slack conversation (the internal chat of many companies), pulled data from several tools and put together a single reply.

Nobody wrote the step-by-step: the role was to answer the team with data.

Why it worksThe context (access to the tools) was already there; the request was the team's question.

3. A compiler

A researcher put sixteen Claude agents to work building a C compiler (the program that turns code written by people into a program the computer can run), with a simple order: keep going until it's perfect.

It took about two weeks of work.

Why it works"Perfect" there had a measure: the compiler passes the tests or it doesn't.

4. The pattern

The requests are short, like the ones you would make to someone on your team. What they have is a way to measure the result and the context at hand.

This is what the role sheet forces you to write.

TakeawayShort request + clear measure + available context = an agent that runs on its own. In the chat, the same sheet makes every conversation start already in the role; running on its own is something for the terminal and automations.

03 · Template: the role sheet

One line per field. Replace the text between < > with your own.

role-sheet.txt
Role: <the job, in one sentence: you take care of ...>
Goal: <what needs to happen>
Measure: <how to know it is finished and good>
Context: <where the rules, the lessons and the files are; why the task exists>
Example: <where the reference work done by you is>
Boundary: <what it must never do>
Check: <when and how you look at the work>
How to hand the sheet to the agent
From now on this is your role. Read the sheet below, the project rules and the example indicated.
Before you start, tell me in three lines how you will check your own work against the Measure.

<paste the sheet here>
Simulation: illustrative example
  • Role: you take care of the school's weekly notice to parents.
  • Goal: every Friday, a notice with what changes the following week.
  • Measure: it fits on one phone screen, gives the date of each change, and I approve it without asking for corrections.
  • Context: rules and lessons in the project instructions; the calendar sent to the project (calendar.pdf).
  • Example: the file sample-notice.txt sent to the project, written by me.
  • Boundary: never promise transportation or mention a student's name.
  • Check: I read it every Friday at 4 p.m. before sending it.
  • Role: you answer the quote requests that come in through the store's WhatsApp.
  • Goal: every quote with items, quantities and a total from the current price list.
  • Measure: total matches the price list, delivery time stated, reply within 1 hour during store hours.
  • Context: price list and shipping rules sent to the project.
  • Example: three quotes I answered myself.
  • Boundary: never give a discount or promise same-day delivery.
  • Check: I look at the day's quotes at 6 p.m.
  • Role: you write the company's job postings.
  • Goal: one posting per open position, based on the manager's request.
  • Measure: fits on one phone screen, gives salary, location and work schedule, and the manager approves it without corrections.
  • Context: job policy and company tone in the project instructions.
  • Example: the warehouse checker posting, written by me.
  • Boundary: never ask for age, photo or marital status.
  • Check: I read it before publishing, the same day.
  • Prestige role ("you are the best copywriter"): it is not a role. A role says what they take care of.
  • Vague measure ("turn out good"): they don't know when to stop.
  • No example: "great" for them is not your "great".
  • In the chat: paste the sheet into the project instructions and send the example as a file. Every new conversation in the project already starts in the role.
  • In the terminal: save it as role-sheet.txt in the folder and mention the file in AGENTS.md.

04 · Check your sheet

Paste your sheet (or upload the .txt). The page checks the seven fields, rejects anything empty or vague and asks that the Measure include a number or a test along with what is being measured ("3 lines", "cites the date", "approved with no corrections"). The OK is a check of form, not a guarantee that the measure is a good one: you are the one who decides, in the practice below.

05 · Practice: the sheet for your first agent

Use the "role candidate" you marked in lesson 1.

E1 · The reference work

30 min

Do the task once yourself, the way you consider excellent. Save it as the reference and write down two mistakes you want to avoid.

Done when: there is a reference file made by you and a list of two mistakes to avoid.

E2 · The sheet

20 min

Fill in role-sheet.txt with the seven fields and paste it into the checker above.

Done when: the checker answers "OK: sheet with all 7 fields filled in" and you wrote, in one sentence, how you check the Measure by looking at a deliverable.

E3 · The first deliverable

20 min

Give the sheet to the agent (in the chat: paste it into the project instructions and open a new conversation; in the terminal: save it in the folder) and ask for the first run using the request from the template.

Done when: the agent described how it will check itself against the Measure and delivered a result that you compared with the reference.

Lesson 1 · 12 min

Three eras: from prompt to role

In just a few years, the way of working with AI has changed three times. First, the request written word by word. Then, the context the AI receives before the request. Now, the role: the AI works like someone on the team and you manage. Knowing which era each of your requests belongs to shows you what's missing.

Start the lesson

Prompt

Scroll the page: each step below lights up its part of the diagram.

  1. 1/4

    Prompt

    The skill was writing the request. Because the model was weak, you made up for it with instructions: "think step by step," "you are an expert," lists of "don't do this." Much of that no longer makes sense with today's models (lesson 2).

  2. 2/4

    Context

    The request got shorter because the AI started receiving, before it, what it needs to know: the rules, the files, the history. The skill became building that foundation once, instead of repeating everything in every conversation (lesson 3).

  3. 3/4

    Role

    The AI runs on its own for hours or days, like an employee. You don't write what it does word for word: you define the role, the goal and the quality standard, and check in from time to time. This is role engineering (lessons 4 to 7).

  4. 4/4

    From the request to management

    With a prompt, you wrote the work. With context, you prepared the foundation. With a role, you manage: you define, check and correct.

One era doesn't erase the one before it: the role uses a clear request and good context. What changes is where your work is.

02 · What each era asks of you

Tap or click each era and compare the same request.

1. Prompt

You describe step by step how to do it, because the model couldn't figure it out on its own.

The result depended more on how you worded the request than on your knowledge of the subject.

Typical requestYou are an award-winning copywriter. Think step by step. Write a post about our course. Don't use emojis. Don't be wordy. Use a friendly tone.

2. Context

Who the audience is, what the house tone is, what has already been published: this is kept in a place the AI reads before answering.

The request stays short because the rest is already there.

Typical requestWrite the launch post for the new course, following the tone and audience in the rules file.

3. Role

You hand over a whole job, not a single task: what success is, how to measure it, where the context is, what good work looks like.

The agent works, checks itself and shows you the result. You review and correct, as you would with someone new on the team.

Typical requestYou take care of promoting the courses. Every Monday, publish one post per new course, following the example post's format. Done when: each post has a link that opens and has been approved by me.

Questions for each row:
- Did I write HOW to do it or WHAT success looks like?
- Did the AI already know the context, or did I repeat everything?
- Could it check on its own whether it was finished?
Simulation: illustrative example
  1. "Summarize this PDF in 5 points" · prompt · it didn't say who the summary is for or what to do with it.
  2. "Write the email in the tone from the rules file" · context · it was missing the done-when criterion (who approves, by when).
  3. "Every Friday by 5 p.m., put together the report just like the example; done when the totals match the spreadsheet and I approve" · role · it has a measurable goal, an example, a deadline and who approves.
  • Thinking a long request is a role request: length doesn't change the era. What changes it is having a measurable goal, saved context and an example.
  • Skipping the "what was missing" column: it is your plan for the next lessons.
